1. Executive Summary
TPT BioCAD is an open-source, unified Computer-Aided Design (CAD) and slicing software platform specifically engineered for bioprinting and food printing. Unlike traditional slicers optimized for thermoplastic FDM/FFF printing, TPT BioCAD natively integrates a computational fluid dynamics (CFD) and rheology solver. This allows users to simulate, optimize, and control the extrusion of complex, non-Newtonian, viscoelastic, and shear-thinning fluids (bio-inks, hydrogels, food pastes, and doughs) prior to and during the physical print.
2. Motivation & Problem Statement
Current open-source slicing software (e.g., Cura, PrusaSlicer) assumes materials are thermoplastics that melt and solidify purely based on temperature. Bioprinting and food printing rely on fundamentally different physical principles:
Bio-inks (e.g., alginate, gelatin methacryloyl) are shear-thinning, often require coaxial cross-linking (e.g., calcium chloride bath), and must maintain cell viability (strict pressure/temperature limits).
Food materials (e.g., chocolate, cookie dough, purees) exhibit yield stress, viscoelasticity, and complex temperature-dependent phase changes (e.g., chocolate tempering).
There is a lack of accessible, open-source software that bridges the gap between geometric CAD design, fluid rheology simulation, and hardware control for these specific domains. TPT BioCAD solves this by treating the "material" as a dynamic fluid system rather than a static thermal profile.
3. Goals and Non-Goals
Goals
Provide a unified UI for CAD design, toolpath generation, and fluid simulation.
Implement a robust Fluid & Rheology Solver capable of modeling non-Newtonian fluids, yield stress, and curing kinetics.
Generate hardware-agnostic G-code (and custom firmware commands) for pneumatic, piston, and screw-based extruders.
Maintain a 100% open-source codebase under the Apache License 2.0 to encourage academic and commercial adoption.
Support multi-material and multi-nozzle printing (e.g., structural support + bio-ink).
Provide safety interlocks that flag or abort a print plan when computed shear stress or thermal exposure exceeds user-configurable material safety thresholds.
Non-Goals
Medical Device Certification: TPT BioCAD is a research and development tool. It does not provide FDA/CE compliance for clinical bioprinting.
General Purpose CFD: The solver is strictly reduced-order and optimized for extrusion-based additive manufacturing, not general-purpose fluid dynamics (like ANSYS Fluent).
Risks & Assumptions
Team & Timeline: This project is being developed solo, part-time. The 12-month roadmap (Section 8) spans a custom CFD/rheology solver, a full B-Rep CAD kernel, a firmware fork, and 5-axis toolpath generation — each of which is independently a substantial undertaking. The roadmap is kept as-is by intent, but phases should be expected to slip without additional contributors.
Hardware & Validation: No physical printer or rheometer is available during early development. Rheology solver accuracy is validated against published literature data and closed-form analytical solutions (see Section 5.2 Validation Strategy) rather than real hardware, until a physical setup is acquired.
CAD Kernel Maturity: opencascade-rs bindings are less mature than the underlying C++ OpenCASCADE library. Full B-Rep support (Section 7) is a hard requirement for this project, but initial integration should be treated as a de-risking spike rather than a solved dependency.
Licensing: OpenCASCADE is distributed under LGPL-2.1 with a static-linking exception, which is compatible with dynamic/static linkage from an Apache 2.0 codebase as a dependency. This should be reconfirmed before any binary distribution.
Simulation Accuracy Disclaimer: Cell viability and food safety predictions from the solver are simulation estimates based on modeled physics, not guarantees — consistent with the Non-Goal that this is not a certified medical device.

5. Core Modules
5.1 TPT-Geometry (CAD & Mesh Processing)
Standard Mesh Import/Export: STL, OBJ, 3MF.
Parametric Lattice Generator: Crucial for bioprinting. Generates internal porous scaffolds (gyroid, honeycomb, custom Voronoi) with variable pore sizes to control nutrient diffusion.
Mesh Repair: Auto-fixing non-manifold edges, specifically tailored for the lower resolution requirements of hydrogel extrusion.
5.2 TPT-Fluid (Physics & Rheology Solver)
This is the core differentiator of TPT BioCAD.
Instead of simple "flow rate" multipliers, TPT-Fluid solves for the actual physics of the material.
Rheological Models: Implements empirical models for complex fluids:
Carreau-Yasuda model for shear-thinning bio-inks.
Herschel-Bulkley model for yield-stress food pastes (e.g., dough).
Bingham plastic model for chocolate.
Extrusion Solver (1D Pipe Flow): Calculates the required pressure (pneumatic) or piston force/velocity to achieve a target volumetric flow rate through a specific nozzle geometry (tapered, coaxial), accounting for wall slip and fluid viscosity.
Deposition & Slumping Simulator (3D Voxel): A simplified, real-time physics simulation that predicts how the extruded bead will spread or slump on the previous layer based on the material's yield stress and the print speed.
Curing/Kinetics Engine: Models UV cross-linking (for GelMA) or thermal setting (for chocolate/agar), adjusting print speeds dynamically to ensure the material sets before the next layer is applied.
Safety Interlocks: Flags or aborts a print plan when computed shear stress (nozzle wall) or thermal exposure exceeds a user-configurable, per-material safety threshold, protecting cell viability and material integrity (see Goals, Section 3).
Validation Strategy: In the absence of physical hardware and a rheometer, the solver is validated in two ways: (1) unit tests against closed-form analytical solutions where they exist (e.g., Newtonian and power-law Poiseuille pipe flow, Herschel-Bulkley plug flow); and (2) regression tests that fit Carreau-Yasuda, Herschel-Bulkley, and Bingham model parameters against published rheometer curves for the materials already listed in the Material Database (Section 6.2), with explicit curve-fit acceptance thresholds (e.g., R²). This validation suite is a required deliverable of Phase 2 (Section 8), not an afterthought.
5.3 TPT-Slice (Toolpath Generation)
Translates geometry and fluid parameters into machine movements.
Dynamic Flow Compensation: Adjusts E-axis (extrusion) or pneumatic pressure on the fly based on nozzle acceleration, preventing under-extrusion at corners.
Multi-Axis Support: 5-axis toolpath generation for printing on curved mandrels (common in vascular bioprinting).
5.4 Hardware Abstraction Layer (HAL)
Standard G-code: Compatible with Marlin, Klipper, and RepRapFirmare. Klipper is the primary, actively-supported target (see 5.5); Marlin/RepRapFirmware support for the extended commands is a later, community-contributed target once the command set is proven on Klipper.
Extended Bio/Food Commands:
M300 S<pressure> P<time>: Pneumatic pressure control.
M301 T<temp> R<ramp_rate>: Advanced thermal profiling (for chocolate tempering).
M302 U<uv_intensity> T<time>: UV curing control.
M303: Coaxial cross-linker flow control.
5.5 Firmware Extension (Klipper)
Implementing M300-M303 requires firmware-side support, which is in scope for this project. Klipper is targeted first because its Python-based `klippy` host software is plugin-extensible without recompiling or re-flashing MCU firmware, making it far more tractable for a solo maintainer than forking Marlin's C++ codebase.
Approach: ship a Klipper Python plugin (`klippy` extra) that registers the M300-M303 G-code commands and maps them onto Klipper's existing primitives (PWM outputs, heater control, output pins) rather than inventing a new MCU-side protocol.
Marlin and RepRapFirmware forks remain open for community contribution once the Klipper implementation has validated the command set in practice.
6. Data Models & File Formats
6.1 The .tpt Project File
A unified project format: a zip archive (following the same pattern as .3mf and .fcstd) containing the following human-readable JSON members:
geometry.json: B-rep or mesh data.
material.json: Rheological parameters (viscosity curves, yield stress, curing rates).
machine.json: Printer kinematics, nozzle diameters, extruder types.
profile.json: Slicing parameters and simulation results.
6.2 Material Database
A built-in, open-source library of pre-characterized materials.
Bio: Alginate (various concentrations), GelMA, Pluronic F-127, Fibrin.
Food: Dark/Milk/White Chocolate, Shortbread dough, Tomato puree, Mashed potato.
Users can input their own rheometer data (CSV) to generate custom material profiles.
7. Technology Stack
To ensure high performance for the fluid solver and cross-platform compatibility, the following stack is proposed:
Core Engine & Solver: Rust. Chosen for memory safety, zero-cost abstractions, and high performance in mathematical computations.
CAD Geometry Kernel: C++ utilizing OpenCASCADE (via Rust bindings like opencascade-rs) for robust B-Rep and lattice generation.
Math/Physics Libraries: nalgebra and ndarray (Rust) for matrix operations and CFD grid calculations.
User Interface: Tauri (Rust backend + Web frontend using React/Svelte) OR Qt/C++ if a more traditional desktop CAD feel is preferred. (Recommendation: Tauri for faster UI iteration and modern look).
Hardware I/O: tokio-serial (Rust) for asynchronous, non-blocking serial communication with printers.
8. Roadmap & Milestones
Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1-3)
Initialize repository, set up CI/CD, and apply Apache 2.0 licensing.
Implement basic UI shell and STL import/export.
Develop basic 3-axis slicer for standard thermoplastics (baseline).
Phase 2: The Fluid Solver (Months 4-7)
Implement Carreau-Yasuda and Herschel-Bulkley rheological models.
Build the 1D extrusion pressure/flow solver.
Integrate material database and UI for rheology parameter input.
Build the solver validation suite: analytical-solution unit tests and literature-curve regression fits (see Section 5.2 Validation Strategy).
Phase 3: Bioprinting Specifics (Months 8-10)
Implement parametric lattice/scaffold generator.
Add UV curing and coaxial needle toolpath support.
Develop "slumping" visualization overlay in the UI.
Phase 4: Food Printing & Hardware (Months 11-12)
Implement thermal profiling algorithms (tempering curves).
Finalize HAL for pneumatic and screw-based extruders.
Implement Klipper plugin for M300-M303 (see Section 5.5).
Beta release and community testing.
